Three Chunk Fixture

Part One

The first paragraph belongs to the opening section. It is deliberately long so the page scrolls: reading aloud turns idle moments into reading time, and a long paragraph gives the reader something to sink into. Sentences follow one another without hurry, each one a small step forward through the argument. The slow web rewards attention, and attention is exactly what spoken text buys back. Keep the eyes on the road, the dishes, or the trail while the words carry on. A paragraph like this one keeps the reader engaged across the fold, so jumping to the next chunk is a real, visible movement on the page. That is the point of this fixture: navigation should move the viewport, not just the state.

The second paragraph is the third chunk of three. It lives at the very bottom of the page, far below the fold, so scrolling it into view is an unmistakable, testable action. When the reader presses forward, the overlay seeks to this chunk and the page scrolls so this text is centered on screen. The smart-back behaviour is measured here too: replay the chunk from its top when more than two seconds have elapsed, or step back to the previous chunk when the button is pressed early. Both motions are deterministic in this fixture because the engine is silent — the coordinator clock decides, not the audio pipeline. Three chunks, three states, one clear path through the article.
