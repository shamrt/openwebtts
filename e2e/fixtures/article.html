The Slow Web

The slow web is a reading-first corner of the internet: long-form essays, hand-written journals, and pages that reward attention instead of competing for it. OpenWebTTS reads these pages aloud so you can keep your eyes on the road, the dishes, or the trail.

Why read aloud

Spoken text turns idle time into reading time. A ten-minute walk becomes a chapter; a commute becomes an essay. The extension's overlay stays out of the page's way until you ask for it, then collapses back to a single pill when you are done.

How it works

A content script extracts the article into speakable chunks, a hybrid text-to-speech engine speaks them, and an as-you-read highlighter tracks the active sentence or paragraph. A skip-to-section slider jumps between headings so you can navigate by structure, not by scroll position.

Engines

The Web Speech API is the primary engine; when it reports no voices, a bundled WASM Piper model takes over so the reader works even where system voices are absent.
